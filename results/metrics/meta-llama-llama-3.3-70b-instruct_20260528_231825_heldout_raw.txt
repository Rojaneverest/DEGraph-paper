Q: `gold/customer_ltv.py` contains TWO aggregations inside its `spark.sql` block. Identify both — their group keys, aggregation functions, and outputs — and describe how each feeds the downstream `ltv_vs_country_avg` and `revenue_quartile` columns.
Score: None

REFERENCE:
Two aggregations, with very different shapes.

(1) `country_avg` CTE — a CLASSIC GROUP BY aggregate. Group key: `country_code` (single key). Aggregate function: `AVG(lifetime_revenue) AS avg_country_ltv`. Source: the `base` CTE which projects 8 columns from `silver.customer_profile`. Output: ONE row per distinct country_code with the per-country mean lifetime_revenue. This aggregate is fully static (no dynamic markers) and the graph should emit a regular AggregatesEdge with `dynamic=False`, `group_keys=['country_code']`, `agg_ops=['avg']`, `output_cols=['avg_country_ltv']`.

Important difference: window aggregates produce one output row PER INPUT ROW, not one per group. Window spec: NO PARTITION BY clause (global ordering across the entire `base` set), order by `lifetime_revenue` DESCENDING. NTILE(4) assigns each row an integer 1..4 representing which quartile of lifetime_revenue it falls into (1 = top quartile, 4 = bottom). The two aggregations are SIBLINGS — neither depends on the other; they both consume `base.lifetime_revenue`. The downstream `ltv_tier` depends only on `revenue_quartile` (and thus only on the window aggregate), not on `ltv_vs_country_avg`.

[HQ8] window-function-impact / medium
Q: The `NTILE(4)` window in `gold/customer_ltv.py` has an ORDER BY but no PARTITION BY. What does this mean semantically, and how would the data semantics of `revenue_quartile` change if you added `PARTITION BY country_code`?
Score: None

REFERENCE:
Current behavior (`OVER (ORDER BY b.lifetime_revenue DESC)` with NO partition): NTILE buckets the ENTIRE customer base into 4 globally-equal-sized groups by descending lifetime_revenue. Quartile 1 = the top 25% of customers by revenue ACROSS THE WHOLE DATASET, regardless of country. A small country with mostly low-revenue customers might have zero quartile-1 customers; a large country might dominate quartile 1. The boundary thresholds are global revenue percentiles.

Graph representation: the DerivesEdge for `revenue_quartile` has `window_spec` with `order_cols=['lifetime_revenue desc']` and `partition_cols=[]` (empty). The empty partition list is the structural fingerprint of a global window.

If changed to `PARTITION BY country_code`: NTILE would bucket WITHIN EACH COUNTRY separately. Every country (with any customers at all) would have exactly some Q1 customers — the top 25% of customers IN THAT COUNTRY by revenue. A high-spending US customer might rank in quartile 1 nationally and quartile 1 globally; a high-spending customer in a tiny country with only 3 other customers would also get quartile 1 even if their absolute revenue is mid-range globally. The graph DerivesEdge would now have `partition_cols=['country_code']`, which is the only structural change.

Downstream impact on `ltv_tier`: the when-chain mapping (quartile 1→platinum, 2→gold, 3→silver, otherwise→bronze) doesn't change syntactically, but the SEMANTIC POPULATION of each tier shifts. With country-partitioned NTILE, EVERY country contributes platinum/gold/silver/bronze customers proportionally — useful for per-country marketing campaigns. With global NTILE, the platinum tier may be heavily concentrated in a few high-spending markets. Same column name, very different business meaning.

The `ltv_vs_country_avg` column is independent of this change — it uses the country_avg AGGREGATE (a group-by), not the NTILE window — so it would not be affected by adding the partition.

MODEL (meta-llama/llama-3.3-70b-instruct):
The `NTILE(4)` window function in `gold/customer_ltv.py` is used to divide the customers into four quartiles based on their `lifetime_revenue` in descending order. Since there is no `PARTITION BY` clause, the window function is applied to the entire result set as a single partition. This means that the quartiles are calculated globally, across all customers, regardless of their country of origin.

If you added `PARTITION BY country_code` to the window function, the semantics would change as follows:

* The customers would be divided into separate partitions based on their `country_code`.
* Within each partition, the customers would be ordered by their `lifetime_revenue` in descending order.
* The `NTILE(4)` function would then assign a quartile to each customer within their respective partition, based on their ranking within that partition.

This would mean that the `revenue_quartile` column would no longer represent a global quartile ranking, but rather a quartile ranking within each country. For example, a customer in the top quartile in one country might not be in the top quartile globally, if there are customers in other countries with higher `lifetime_revenue` values.

In terms of data semantics, the addition of `PARTITION BY country_code` would:

* Allow for more nuanced analysis of customer revenue patterns within each country.
* Enable identification of high-revenue customers within each country, rather than just globally.
* Change the interpretation of the `revenue_quartile` column, which would now represent a country-specific ranking rather than a global ranking.

Overall, the addition of `PARTITION BY country_code` would provide a more detailed and country-specific view of customer revenue patterns, but would also change the meaning of the `revenue_quartile` column in a way that might require adjustments to downstream analysis or decision-making processes.
