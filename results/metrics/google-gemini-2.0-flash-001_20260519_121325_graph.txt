[Q5] join-chain / hard
Q: In `silver/refunds_enriched.py`, the result joins three bronze tables. List the join keys for each join and explain what happens if `return_id` becomes nullable in `bronze.refunds_raw`.
Score: None

REFERENCE:
Two joins in the file: (1) `refunds_raw` ⨝ `returns_raw` ON `return_id` (inner join), (2) the result ⨝ `orders_raw` ON `order_id` (left join). If `return_id` becomes nullable in `refunds_raw`, the inner join on `return_id` silently drops every refund row with null return_id — silver.refunds_enriched will under-report refund volume. The downstream `refund_ratio = refund_amount / total_amount` derivation cannot compute for those dropped rows. Because the second join is LEFT, the orders side does not introduce additional row loss, but `total_amount` becomes NULL for orphan-order refunds. Net effect: any analytics on refund volume or refund-to-order ratio become biased toward the subset of refunds that arrived with a non-null return_id.

MODEL (google/gemini-2.0-flash-001):
The `silver/refunds_enriched.py` script joins three bronze tables: `refunds_raw`, `returns_raw`, and `orders_raw`.

1.  The first join is an inner join between `refunds_raw` and `returns_raw` on the join key `return_id`.
2.  The second join is a left join between the result of the first join and `orders_raw` on the join key `order_id`.

If `return_id` becomes nullable in `refunds_raw`, the inner join between `refunds_raw` and `returns_raw` will not include rows where `refunds_raw.return_id` is null. This will result in fewer rows in the `refunds_with_return` dataframe, and consequently, fewer refunds will be enriched. The rows with null `return_id` in `refunds_raw` will be dropped from the `refunds_enriched` table.
